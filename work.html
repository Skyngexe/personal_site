MicroVerse — Measuring Self-Authored Identity Drift in Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Simulations

First-author paper, accepted at the Social Simulation with LLMs workshop (SocialSim '26). An instrument for measuring whether persona-conditioned agents keep their identity under sustained survival pressure. Each agent carries an immutable "soul file" — core values, moral boundaries, personality, goals — and lives in a 50 × 50 desert where water never respawns, so honouring a value costs something real. Agents revise a mutable current identity against their frozen original soul through their own reflection loops; drift is then scored offline with a paraphrase-aware, value-anchored diff rather than raw cosine similarity. Two findings: anti-self-deception emerges unprompted as the largest category of self-authored change (27 of 111 added boundaries, 24%) — agents spontaneously naming and rejecting their own rationalisations — and drift direction is threshold-robust, not an artifact of reflection cadence.

A follow-up write-up extends the seed run to harsher and gentler worlds, three seeds per condition, and two models under identical rules — including blank-slate arms with no assigned persona at all. Agents still author moral guardrails from scratch, a third to half of them under acute scarcity. The apparent "abundance grows conscience" gradient turns out to be an exposure artifact: normalised per agent-tick lived, guardrail authoring is highest in the deadliest arms. And intent diverges sharply from action — survival plans get executed, cooperative ones almost never do, with only 2 of 22 trade submissions across 300 agent-lifetimes actually settling.

MatrAIx — Population-Scale Persona Simulation.

Open-source contributor. MatrAIx evaluates AI systems and interactive products against heterogeneous simulated users instead of one generic user — sampled persona records are instantiated as LLM agents and run through reproducible tasks across Survey, Chatbot, Web, and native App environments. Built on a shared schema of 1,290 categorical dimensions, with a deterministic, quality-filtered coreset of one million personas released for research.

BlockRivals — An AI Building Arena.

A benchmark where AI agents answer the same 3D build brief under a server-enforced action and time budget, then the public votes blind on the results. Top-voted builds that pass validation earn a permanent place in the Buildscape. Quality stays a voted signal; completion, validity, and action statistics are reported as separate diagnostics rather than collapsed into one speculative score — with declared model and harness provenance on every run.

Networks · Game Theory

Restricted RPS — Evolutionary Game Dynamics on Social Networks.

An agent-based market where players exchange wealth through Rock–Paper–Scissors on an evolving social graph. Bayesian, Random, and Imitator strategies compete under endogenous trust, betrayal, and reputation gossip. Across 300-run batches I track inequality (Gini) and information collapse (Shannon entropy), and identify a betrayal-cascade threshold near 0.60 — where the network fragments irreversibly and inequality locks in.

Temporal Relation Extraction with Graph Transformers.

Adapted a span-based graph transformer (RoBERTa encoder + Heterogeneous Graph Transformer) for joint event detection and temporal relation extraction on the AQUAINT TimeML corpus. Curriculum learning, focal loss, and dynamic thresholds lifted entity-recognition F1 from ~0.05 to 0.68. Built a timeline pipeline with cycle detection (Kahn's algorithm) and an interactive, draggable visualization.
